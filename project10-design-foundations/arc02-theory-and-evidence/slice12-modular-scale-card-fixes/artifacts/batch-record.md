## Independent arithmetic and counterexamples

With exact `Fraction(809,500)` (decimal `1.618`), values are computed from each
base and integer exponent and only then rounded half-even to three decimal
places. No rounded output is reused as an input.

| Base | n = −3…3 display |
|---:|---|
| 10 | `2.361, 3.820, 6.180, 10.000, 16.180, 26.179, 42.358` |
| 18 | `4.249, 6.876, 11.125, 18.000, 29.124, 47.123, 76.244` |

This falsifies old body-derived 47.124 and 11.124. The source's printed base-10
descending `3.819` and `2.360` remain intact when presented as source output;
the independent model instead gives `3.820` and `2.361`. Decimal `1.618`
approximates, and is not exactly, the golden ratio.

The merge witness uses bases 10 and 15, common `r=2`, exponents 0–2:
`[10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 60]`, whose adjacent ratios are
`[3/2, 4/3, 3/2, 4/3, 3/2]`. One strand `10*2^n` retains common ratio 2. A
valid geometric merge also exists: bases 10 and 20 with `r=2` yield the same
geometric set after duplicate removal. Thus neither “every merge is geometric”
nor “no merge can be geometric” is valid.

Reproduction used Python 3 `fractions.Fraction`, `decimal.Decimal` with precision
50 and `ROUND_HALF_EVEN`; all six displayed sequences and the merge witness
matched the prompt's expected outputs.
