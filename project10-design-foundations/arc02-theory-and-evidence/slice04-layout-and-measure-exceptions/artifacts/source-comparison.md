## Clause-to-evidence application

- **Measure and font units:** CV supports the bounded unit definition; SZ supports which sizing properties and box edges are involved. Neither establishes 60ch as universally appropriate. CV §10.2, consulted for C10.01, defines comparison-function behavior including `clamp()`; it does not select a design value. The toy calculation in the handoff is arithmetic under stipulated advances, not actual browser wrapping.
- **Flexible layout:** FX supports flex sizing/wrapping mechanics subject to algorithm inputs. It cannot support the policy conclusion that a task remains operable after a group wraps; that is a distinct local design claim and must be checked against a concrete task.
- **Grid and content sizing:** SZ and the consulted GR clauses support that track functions and definite sizing conditions constrain auto-repeat. They do not entail universal auto-fit, universally fluid height, or the particular “minimum acceptable” track size. Those are local recommendations needing stated context and review.
- **Box model:** SZ §3.3 explains content-box/border-box and their relation to sizing properties. It does not warrant a claim that padding never changes an element’s outside size.
- **Container units:** CQ §6 is required for C26’s container-unit claim; §5.1 alone was insufficient. Eligible container, axis and fallback conditions are distinct from deciding whether a query or threshold is warranted. No support matrix was tested.
- **Guarantees and accessibility:** EL attribution is kept separate from technical truth. Standards provide bounded mechanisms/requirements, not guaranteed outcomes across unanticipated content/context or proof of local conformance. Representative and adverse cases remain necessary.
