### Q1: What are the gestalt principles of perceptual grouping, and how does each one work?

Nearby marks can group by proximity; matching appearances can group by similarity; crossing strokes can continue as two paths rather than turn into each other; partial outlines can suggest closed regions; boundary ownership separates figure from ground. Subdivision articulates regions while whole/part context affects their appearance. These cues can compete and depend on the task.

Contribution boundary / missing components: The17 supply continuation, similarity, completion and subdivision examples, plus framework/category/axis accounts. Framework-only members do not each supply every principle; the set is not a complete modern catalog. Figure-ground is external premise-only support; source-specific Musatti reduction is attributed.

### Q2: How does the relationship between inter-group and intra-group spacing communicate which elements belong together (gestalt proximity)?

Compare within-group gaps with between-group separation: smaller internal gaps and larger separation can suggest units, while shared colour, enclosure, alignment or continuation can reinforce or override spacing. Identical dots can still form separated clusters; no fixed4px/24px gap or mandatory ratio follows.

Contribution boundary / missing components: similarity, subdivision and continuation give concrete contributing cues. This conceptual answer needs no invented screenshot requirement; a particular rendered spacing outcome still needs actual content and observation.

### Q3: An icon set mixes outlined and filled styles, some at 16px and some at 24px, some with rounded corners and some with sharp corners. It feels 'off.' What principle was violated?

The candidate problem is unintended family incoherence: differences in stroke/fill, optical size, corner character or weight may communicate inconsistent roles. Determine which properties should be shared and which differences serve meaning; mixing16px/24px or outlined/filled forms is not automatically a violated law.

Contribution boundary / missing components: contour,shape,axes,similarity,economy support this distinction. No actual icon set or rendered usability has been inspected; the hypothetical principle can still be answered directly.

### Q4: How does cognitive load theory (intrinsic, extraneous, germane) relate to progressive disclosure and visual hierarchy? When is simplification harmful?

Simplification is harmful when it removes distinctions, labels, context, grouping or cues needed to understand and act; an apparently clean part can make the whole less intelligible. These cards support that criterion.

Contribution boundary / missing components: This cohort does NOT explain intrinsic/extraneous/germane cognitive-load taxonomy or how progressive disclosure manages task complexity. ### Q5: A developer says 'I tried making it look good by adding more colours.' The result looks chaotic. What foundational principle are they missing?

Colour variety needs a purpose in the role structure and hierarchy. Compare whether distinctions serve content and action; more colours alone do not improve coherence, and fewer colours alone do not ensure it. Keep expressive variation when it helps and check complete real pairs.

Contribution boundary / missing components: parsimony,praegnanz,simplicity supply economy/coherence; current colour guide supplies role and state-pair methods separately. No fixed palette count or empirical result is inferred.

### Q6: How do you systematically audit an existing interface for visual design quality? What checklist of principles and patterns do you evaluate, in what order?

A useful pass states brief,task,content and supplied identity; compares the whole and its parts for hierarchy/grouping; inspects type,colour and layout choices; and checks the relevant interaction/accessibility behavior. Iterate where a local improvement hurts the whole. The order can change with the task or an observed defect.

Contribution boundary / missing components: These cohort joins supply only whole/part,economy and axis diagnostics; they do not establish a complete audit checklist or mandatory order. The actual current SKILL Review and design/type/colour methods provide separate support for broader checks, not all-card CQ completeness or fresh behavior proof.

### Q7: What is visual hierarchy, and what visual properties (size, colour, contrast, weight, spacing) establish it?

Visual hierarchy communicates relative importance and sequence. Size,colour,contrast,weight and spacing can distinguish roles; grouping/subdivision identifies units within which those differences are interpreted. Nesting alone does not communicate importance, and equal peers can correctly have equal treatment.

Contribution boundary / missing components: The recorded parsimony/subdivision/whole-part memberships supply economy,nested units and contextual relations. Current type role/hierarchy and colour role methods separately address the named properties; no claim that these three cards alone cover every property or prove a rendered hierarchy.
