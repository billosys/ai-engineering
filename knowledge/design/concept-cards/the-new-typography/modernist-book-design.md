# Quick Definition

The recorded modernist book programme proposes alternatives to "a book page of even grey" through "contrasting typographic and photographic elements using brightness, space, and direction." Its retained exception matters: novels and scientific literature can keep conventional book structures. Integration and affordable broad communication do not require every long-form page to become a photographic collage.

# Core Definition

This section preserves the card’s recorded account of Tschichold’s historical programme; quotations, attributions and page claims have not been checked against the unavailable represented edition. Its polemics are not universal contemporary rules.

Tschichold describes a revolution: "In the area of book design, in the last few years a revolution has taken place... It means placing much greater emphasis on the appearance of the book and a wholly contemporary use of typographic and photographic means."

The formal principle: "The Renaissance ideal — a book page of even grey — is finished; instead we have harmony from contrasting typographic and photographic elements using brightness, space, and direction. Contemporary bindings and book-jackets similarly show an increasing use of photography."

He traces the development from Marinetti's typographic experiments through Lissitzky's poetry books to Heartfield's photomontage covers, establishing that "blocks, whether from photographs or drawings, have become an integrated part of the book" — where previously "the artists of the pre-war period denied the possibility of artistic unity in books using halftone blocks."

The practical demands: "(1) a contemporary (therefore not historical) typographic form; (2) inexpensive books for people, not luxury books for snobs."

# Key Properties

The historical preferences below describe the programme; current use depends on content, audience, language/script, output, constraints and supplied identity.

1. **Contextual contrast**: Type/image/space contrast can serve appropriate books; sustained prose may retain even grey texture and stable centered structures.
2. **Photography as book element**: "The photographic block is being introduced into the book as a new element."
3. **Reported type preference**: The programme favors sans-serif display and allows functional roman text. Contemporary books can use supplied serif or sans-serif faces appropriate to script and reading duration.
4. **Recorded DIN choices**: A5 handbooks, A4 art books and A6 smaller books are examples, not mandatory sizes. Production, content and use can justify other formats.
5. **Economy over luxury**: Affordable books for broad readership, not precious objects for collectors.
6. **Unprinted space as element**: "Unprinted space, which formerly meant nothing... is now as much a proper element as printed matter."

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct/Create:
1. Identify reading duration, content structure, audience, script, budget and supplied identity; retain a conventional novel or scientific-book structure where it serves the work.
2. Choose a format from actual production and content needs; DIN is an option rather than a requirement.
3. Compare supplied faces, including serifs, with representative long prose and headings; preserve useful texture and hierarchy.
4. Where images support the content, integrate type and images deliberately. Stable grey prose and centered substructures remain available.
5. Select photography, illustration or ornament for the book’s communicative, cultural and affective purposes; compare affordability and output quality.

## To Identify/Recognise:
1. Recognize the programme’s proposed integration and contrast without turning asymmetry, photography or DIN into a checklist for every book.
2. Centered layout, even grey pages and historical typefaces can serve the programme’s retained novel/scientific-book exception as well as contemporary reading needs.

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: Book design, publication layout, editorial design, cover design.
- **Common applications**: Contemporary book and magazine layout, digital publication design, e-book design, web-based long-form content.
- **Historical note (reported, unverified here)**: Key practitioners cited: Marinetti (typographic experimentation, 1919), Lissitzky (Mayakovsky poetry book, 1922-23), Heartfield (photomontage covers for Malik), Teige (Czech book design), Moholy-Nagy (Bauhaus books). Tschichold later moderated his views in *Typographische Gestaltung* (1935), moving toward a more classical approach while retaining the principle of functional design.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Applying modernist surface features to fundamentally traditional book structures.
  **Correction**: Tschichold insists the traditional book form "is perfectly suitable for this kind of book" (novels, scientific literature) — only modify where genuine improvement is possible, not for novelty's sake.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: The "new book" means every book must be radically redesigned.
  **Clarification**: "It is not a question of making a really new kind of book (in a technical sense), because the old book-form is perfectly suitable for this kind of book" — modification is appropriate; revolution is for books whose content demands it (artist's books, photography books).
