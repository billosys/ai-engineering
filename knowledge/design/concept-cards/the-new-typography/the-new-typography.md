# Quick Definition

The New Typography is a modernist programme (reported c. 1925-1930) advocating a turn from decorative, centered typography toward functional, asymmetric design whose form grows out of the content's communicative purpose — "The essence of the New Typography is clarity."

# Core Definition

This section preserves the card’s recorded account of Tschichold’s historical programme; quotations, attributions and page claims have not been checked against the unavailable represented edition. Its polemics are not universal contemporary rules.

Tschichold's manifesto defines the movement through its core thesis: "The essence of the New Typography is clarity. This puts it into deliberate opposition to the old typography whose aim was 'beauty' and whose clarity did not attain the high level we require today."

The movement originated from the collective work of avant-garde artists across Europe — Baumeister, Bayer, Lissitzky, Moholy-Nagy, Schwitters, Zwart — who applied the formal principles of abstract painting and constructivism to typography. As Tschichold states: "The break with the old typography, made complete by the new movement, means nothing less than the total discarding of decorative concepts and the turn to functional design."

The New Typography addresses the conditions of modern communication: "Modern man has to absorb every day a mass of printed matter... The speed with which the modern consumer of printing has to absorb it means that the form of printing also must adapt itself to the conditions of modern life."

# Key Properties

The historical preferences below describe the programme; current use depends on content, audience, language/script, output, constraints and supplied identity.

1. **Programme’s priority**: Clarity is presented as the primary goal. Contemporary communication can also serve beauty, cultural meaning and decoration.
2. **Functional form**: "The New Typography is distinguished from the old by the fact that its first objective is to develop its visible form out of the functions of the text."
3. **Asymmetric advocacy**: The programme favors non-axial composition; centered structures remain available for suitable tasks.
4. **Contemporary expression**: "Every piece of typography which originates in a preconceived idea of form, of whatever kind, is wrong."
5. **Collective, not individual**: The movement emerged from collective effort, not individual genius.
6. **Not a fashion**: "It would be naive and short-sighted to think that the New Typography... is only a temporary fashion."

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct/Create:
1. For a study or deliberate adaptation of this approach, identify content, purpose, audience, script, output, constraints and supplied identity.
2. Consider asymmetric spatial grouping where it supports hierarchy; script-appropriate line alignment and meaningful source/focus order are separate choices.
3. Study the programme’s ornament rejection without adopting a blanket modern ban; decoration can serve cultural, affective and identity purposes.
4. Deploy contrast (size, weight, colour) to express the logical structure of the text.
5. Treat white space as an active design element.

## To Identify/Recognise:
1. Non-axial or dynamic page composition is a historical cue; left-aligned text alone is not asymmetry.
2. Recorded visual cues include sans-serif, size contrast, rules and colour; these are not mandatory for every contemporary design.
3. Integration of photography with typography.
4. The programme’s omission of decorative ornament is a historical cue, not a universal standard of modernity.

# Context & Application

- **Typical contexts**: All forms of printed communication — letterheads, posters, books, advertisements, periodicals.
- **Common applications**: The principles remain foundational to modern graphic design and web design.
- **Historical note (reported, unverified here)**: Published June 1928 in Munich. Tschichold later moderated his position, acknowledging the value of traditional typography for certain contexts (notably book design), but the movement's core principles — clarity, function, asymmetry — became the foundation of 20th-century graphic design.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Adopting external features (bold rules, circles, triangles) as new decoration.
  **Correction**: "If these are merely substituted for the old ornaments, nothing is improved." This is the programme’s criticism of copying a surface style; contemporary ornament may serve cultural, affective and identity purposes.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: The New Typography is a style or fashion to be copied.
  **Clarification**: "Merely to copy its external shapes would be to create a new formalism as bad as the old." It is a method of working from function to form, not a set of visual rules.
