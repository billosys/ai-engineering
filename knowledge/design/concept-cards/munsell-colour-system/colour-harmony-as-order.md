# Quick Definition

“Colour harmony as order” names the recorded Cleland preference for orderly colour relationships. It can organize palette exploration but does not prove that every regular path is pleasing or usable.

# Core Definition

The earlier extraction attributes the following wording to Cleland; the book and prepared text are unavailable, so quotations, locators, context and historical attribution remain unverified.

Retained reported wording: "'color harmony' is only another term for color order; that order will yield order; and that any path in the Color Sphere, and some paths outside it, which are themselves orderly in form and interval, will lead through a series of colors which accord, and when used together will render the agreeable sensation which we seek in all color relations"

This is the reported thesis of orderly selection, not a general perceptual law. Named schemes can describe paths, but continuity does not imply smoothness or equal appearance and mathematical regularity does not guarantee pleasure or usability.

# Key Properties

1. The claim “harmony is order” is attributed aesthetic advice, not a universal equivalence.
2. Named methods can be represented by paths in a chosen diagram; that representation does not supply a perceptual metric.
3. Continuity alone implies neither smoothness, equal perceived steps nor pleasure.
4. Fixed hue with varying value/chroma is movement in both height and radius, not only vertical motion.
5. A circle's arc length, straight-line Euclidean distance and perceptual difference are distinct quantities.
6. No orderly path guarantees realizability, contrast, accessible states or coherent roles.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Inferring that every orderly path is harmonious and every irregular palette discordant.
  **Correction**: Treat the path as a design proposal and assess the actual task and viewing result.
- **Error**: Rejecting individual colours or many hues solely because they do not follow a regular path.
  **Correction**: Identify actual role, hierarchy or pair problems; regularity is optional.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: Numeric order or continuity proves equal appearance.
  **Clarification**: Neither supplies the necessary perceptual calibration or metric.
- **Confusion**: Mathematical organization guarantees quality.
  **Clarification**: It can make choices inspectable; quality remains a contextual evaluation.
