# Quick Definition

Arnheim’s simplicity account explains some depth readings as interpretations of a flat pattern that yield a simpler three-dimensional organization. It is a theoretical framework, not a demonstrated universal mechanism for all depth perception.

# Core Definition

This presents the represented Arnheim account alongside current analytic qualifications. Original edition, pages, quotations, figures, experiments, physiology and historical claims remain unassessed; the technical constructions below do not certify source fidelity.

The represented account calls depth an avenue of freedom: several spatial arrangements can project to the same flat pattern, and a regular 3D candidate may compete with an irregular flat one. A parallelogram can suggest a tilted rectangle, interrupted contours can suggest complete overlapping forms, and figure-ground can organize bounded and continuing regions. These are interpretations with conditions, not evidence that knowledge, learning or other cues never matter. A flat candidate can also be compelling, and completion need not recover a unique hidden form.

Retained wording from the represented account (original fidelity unassessed):

"Why do we see depth?"

"a pattern will appear three-dimensional when it can be seen as the projection of a three-dimensional situation that is structurally simpler than the two-dimensional one"

# Key Properties

1. 3D interpretations of a 2D image are generally underdetermined.
2. Simplicity compares candidate organizations under an account and context.
3. Regularity of shape can trade against simplicity of orientation.
4. The same description does not establish one neural mechanism for overlap, gradients and stereo.

# Construction / Recognition

## To Construct/Create:

1. Compare a flat reading with a spatial reading of the same pattern.
2. Identify which contour continuation or regularity favors each.
3. Use an abstract or naturalistic treatment as appropriate; neither guarantees a stronger result.

## To Identify/Recognise:

1. Name the proposed simpler spatial candidate.
2. Inspect whether the actual context also supports a flat or different spatial reading.
3. In an audit, flag inconsistent unintended spatial interpretations for task review rather than treating simplicity as a score.

# Examples

Source-specific examples and quotations below are retained as reports with their original wording and locators. They are not independently checked historical, experimental or universal claims. Applied examples are qualified design proposals.

**Example 1** (p. 276): "The basic principle of depth perception derives from the law of simplicity and indicates that a pattern will appear three-dimensional when it can be seen as the projection of a three-dimensional situation that is structurally simpler than the two-dimensional one." — Arnheim

**Example 2** (p. 268): "The third dimension is therefore an 'avenue of freedom,' which allows for changes in the interest of simplification of structure." — Arnheim

**Example 3** (p. 270): "The answer may seem strange... the three-dimensionality of vision seems to offer no problem—until we recall that the optical input for all our visual experience consists in the two-dimensional projection on the retina." — Arnheim, noting the paradox that must be explained.

**Example 4** (p. 396): "A pattern will appear three-dimensional when it can be seen as the projection of a three-dimensional situation that is structurally simpler than the two-dimensional one. The frontal figure is seen as a projection only when the resulting three-dimensional shape is structurally simpler." — Arnheim, restating the principle in the context of shape perception.

# Common Errors

- **Error**: Assuming any pattern that permits a simpler 3D explanation must look deep.
  **Correction**: Compare visible cues and context; the account does not guarantee the chosen interpretation or rank geometric patterns over photographs.

# Common Confusions

- **Confusion**: A reported Ames-room or simplicity example proves perception is unlearned.
  **Clarification**: A compelling but physically incorrect reading can illustrate the account without settling learning, physiology or all depth cues.
