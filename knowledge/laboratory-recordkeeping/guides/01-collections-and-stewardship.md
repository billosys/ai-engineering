# Collections and stewardship

A research collection can hold work on unrelated questions under common
governance. The collection's conventions provide continuity across people,
tools, and subjects. Scientific coherence is needed only where the inquiry
itself calls for it.

## Scope and vocabulary

Use ordinary language in daily work. A **lab** is a continuing home for an
area of inquiry or a maintained experimental capability. An **experiment**
is a bounded investigation; a **run** is one execution attempt. A **journal**
records the chronology of thinking and work across those boundaries.

The archival terms help explain the design without becoming required labels:

- **Holdings:** the contents held by a repository.
- **Record group:** records grouped by administrative origin or provenance.
- **Fonds:** records accumulated through the activities of a particular creator.

Common custody does not establish common origin. Record who created materials
separately from who now maintains them. These distinctions are drawn from the
[Society of American Archivists](../references/sources-and-adaptations.md#archival-terminology).

## Establish a small stewardship record

The collection README identifies its purpose, governing body, maintainer or
contact route, adopted conventions, and lab index. Each lab names its scope,
maintainer, status, and any departures from the collection defaults. Link
shared guidance rather than maintaining competing copies.

Decide where records live, what can be published, and who will retain external
evidence before collecting it. Review those arrangements when a lab is paused
or retired. CERN treats research data management as a lifecycle extending
before and after active research; our lightweight adaptation is a storage and
retention statement in the lab README. [Source](https://openscience.cern/research-data-management-open-data/)

Local defaults may be overridden explicitly: name the scope, reason, date,
and responsible person in the nearest README. An override changes placement
or procedure without silently weakening the evidence a claim needs.

## Preserve the intellectual history

Retain decisions, abandoned approaches, unresolved questions, and problems
when they explain how work developed. CERN's archival guidance includes
research notebooks and working notes, emphasizing their value in explaining
decisions. Our application is to retain dated context alongside results.
[Source](https://library.cern/archives/your_records/guide_archival_material)

An engineering deliverable arising from research should link to the appropriate
engineering plan. Its acceptance does not follow from a successful experiment;
likewise, an implemented feature does not establish a scientific claim.
