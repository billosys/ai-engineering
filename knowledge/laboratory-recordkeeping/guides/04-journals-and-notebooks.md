# Journals and notebooks

A journal is the chronological account of the inquiry. It can contain notes
from reading, conversations, observations, calculations, decisions, and ideas
that never become experiments. The experiment record organizes one question;
the journal explains how questions and interpretations developed.

## Write near the event

Use the [journal template](../templates/journal-entry.md). Record author,
event time, writing time if different, context, and links to relevant work.
Separate:

- observations or source statements;
- interpretation and assumptions;
- exploratory and tentative ideas;
- decisions, their reasons, and open questions.

Short entries are welcome. Preserve enough context for someone without the
conversation to understand what prompted the note. Reference precise pages,
sections, file revisions, or run paths when they matter. Mark a reconstruction
from memory as retrospective and state its uncertainty.

## Correct without erasing

Before sharing or using an entry as evidence, ordinary drafting edits are fine.
Afterward, material corrections get a dated addendum explaining the old claim,
the correction, and its basis. Link the correction from affected reports and
indexes. Git preserves revisions but does not tell a reader why a conclusion
changed; that explanation belongs in the record.

Raw captures remain unchanged. A corrected transcription or cleaned dataset is
a derived artifact linked to its source. If material must be removed for an
actual access or disclosure reason, record the removal and resulting evidence
gap in the public inventory without reproducing the restricted content.

## Native notebooks and conversations

Keep native computational files alongside readable exports when their format
is awkward for readers or LLMs. Record which file is authoritative and which
is an export. A displayed notebook result may be stale or produced out of
order; preserve execution order and initialization state. Follow
[storage and preservation](./06-storage-and-preservation.md) for Maxima,
Octave, and other computational environments.

For an LLM-assisted session, identify participants/models when known and
preserve the task packet, relevant tool results, and consequential edits.
Conversation excerpts can support a journal entry; they do not replace raw
experiment outputs. Do not treat an assistant's self-report as evidence that
it read a file, ran a command, or verified a result.

## Equipment and setup logs

Record setup changes and maintenance as dated entries linked to the applicable
procedure revision. If checking whether a setup works is the inquiry itself,
use a commissioning experiment with observable criteria and runs. This keeps
operational history distinct from evidence that the setup passed those checks.
