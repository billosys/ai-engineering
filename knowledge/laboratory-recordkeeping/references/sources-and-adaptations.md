# Sources and adaptations

Consulted 2026-09-22. This register preserves source identity and the specific
ideas adopted; it is not a local mirror of the source documents. Sources retain
their own terms. Our filenames, templates, statuses, numbering, and defaults
are Billo conventions. No CERN affiliation, endorsement, or policy compliance
is implied.

## CERN archival records

[Quick guide to Archival and Non-Archival Material](https://library.cern/archives/your_records/guide_archival_material),
CERN Archives, sections “Archival Material” and appraisal questions.

The guide includes notebooks, experiment logbooks, setup records, and working
notes, and values context behind decisions. It asks that original ordering be
preserved during archival transfer. We adapt this into provenance-aware intake,
dated corrections, and preservation of meaningful unsuccessful work; this does
not prohibit creating navigation or documented migrations.

## CERN analysis preservation

[Frequently Asked Questions](https://analysispreservation.cern.ch/docs/general/faq.html),
CERN Analysis Preservation, especially “How do you define an analysis?”

CAP describes analysis resources broadly: data, code, workflows, containers,
configuration, and contextual metadata. We adopt the dependency trail from a
finding to its underlying materials. Using CAP itself is not required.

## CERN reproducibility

[Reproducible Analysis](https://openscience.cern/reproducibility/), CERN Open
Science Office.

This page connects analysis preservation with executable workflows and REANA,
and points readers to wider reproducibility resources. We adopt environment
and execution capture while permitting plain command recipes and native tools.
A preserved recipe is not evidence of a successful independent rerun.

## CERN research data management

[Research Data Management & Open Data](https://openscience.cern/research-data-management-open-data/),
CERN Open Science Office, sections on research data management and plans.

RDM spans preparation, active collection, processing, release, and long-term
preservation. We adapt the lifecycle into storage, access, retention, and
retrieval decisions appropriate to each lab. The page links a CERN DMP template;
we have not adopted or reproduced that template, nor imposed funder rules.

## Institutional scope

[CERN Archives](https://archives.cern.ch/index.php/cern-archives) describes
holdings spanning scientific, technical, and administrative activity.
[CERN Grey Book](https://greybook.cern.ch/home/content) describes a registry of
research experiments/projects, institutions, and participants. These illustrate
the distinction between a programme of activities and its retained records.
Our collection can hold unrelated inquiries under shared stewardship.

## Archival terminology

Society of American Archivists, Dictionary of Archives Terminology:

- [Record group](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/record-group.html):
  grouping grounded in administrative origin or provenance.
- [Fonds](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/fonds.html): records organically
  accumulated through a creator's activities.
- [Holdings](https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/holdings.html): a repository's
  collective contents.

We use these distinctions to keep custody and creation separate. Contributors
need not use archival vocabulary in directory names or ordinary entries.

## Local method boundaries

[Scientific-methods](https://github.com/billosys/ai-engineering/tree/main/knowledge/scientific-methods)
owns inquiry design, operational measures, and interpretation. This skill adds
record organization and preservation. The lab/experiment/run profile, inbox
dispositions, templates, and native-notebook handling are our synthesis for
human and LLM navigation, informed by the sources above and the intended
computational research use cases.
