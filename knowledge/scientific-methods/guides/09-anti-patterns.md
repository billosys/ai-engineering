# Scientific Method Anti-Patterns

Use these anti-patterns as a quick check before running or accepting an
experiment.

## Anti-Patterns

**Changing several variables and naming one.** If prompt, model, tools, task,
and time budget all change, the result is a bundle comparison.

**Scoring after seeing the answer.** A rubric invented after the result will
often reward the result that feels right.

**Protocol theater.** The protocol looks precise but does not say what evidence
would change the decision.

**Overbroad task.** The test becomes the real project and loses its comparison
value.

**Hidden contamination.** A run imports knowledge from another condition,
installed skill, prior conversation, or operator hint without recording it.

**Metric substitution.** The evaluation measures what is easy to count instead
of what matters.

**No baseline.** A regression claim is made without preserving the behavior the
new result should be compared against.

**Summary as evidence.** The conclusion cites a narrative report without
preserving the artifacts, commands, outputs, or examples behind it.

**Vague improvement.** "Better" is claimed without saying better at what, for
whom, and under what task conditions.

**Erased limitations.** Known confounds and protocol deviations disappear from
the final conclusion.

## Recovery Moves

- Reframe the question.
- Reduce the scope.
- Name the bundle honestly.
- Predeclare the rubric.
- Preserve the raw artifacts.
- Separate observation from interpretation.
- Report the limitation before the conclusion.
