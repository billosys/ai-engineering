## Sampling Rationale

Chapter 1 supplies the conceptual-principles case: it asks whether a record can
retain a qualified explanatory claim without collapsing the distinction between
a computational approach, observed behavior, and emergent explanation. Chapter
7 supplies the memory case: it asks whether a record can preserve a mechanism,
its distinctions, and its cited or figure-dependent context without treating
the surrounding chapter as undifferentiated evidence.

Together the sample deliberately exposes different failure modes while staying
small enough for full operator review. It is not statistically representative
of the book and must not be described as a corpus-wide quality result.

## Candidate Pressure To Observe

| Pressure | Chapter 1 expectation | Chapter 7 expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim granularity | Principles may be broad and qualified. | Mechanisms may couple several necessary distinctions. |
| Support boundaries | Citations and cross-chapter references can be contextual rather than direct support. | Figures and cited experimental framing can be necessary to interpret a claim. |
| Relationships | Conceptual dependencies may need typed links rather than duplication. | Part/whole, causal, and contrast relationships should remain explicit. |
| Competency questions | Questions should test explanation rather than term recall. | Questions should test discrimination between related memory mechanisms. |

## Scope Limits

The execution may retain only a small, operator-reviewable candidate set drawn
from these sections. It must not sweep all ten chapters, resolve the entire
bibliography, or process all 131 figures. A direct inspection of a specific
figure or cited material is allowed only when a selected candidate depends on
it, and must be recorded as a dependency review rather than disguised as a
larger corpus pass.
