# Concept Card: Recognition Dual Process

## Concept Boundary

This card covers the source's distinction between familiarity and recollection
in recognition memory. It excludes the full recognition-memory literature and
any external study review.

## Quick Definition And Core Definition

Quick definition: dual-process recognition distinguishes knowing that something
is familiar from recollecting contextual details about it.

Core definition: the source treats recognition as not merely weaker recall.
Familiarity can support a sense that an item was encountered before, while
recollection retrieves associated contextual information.

## Prerequisites And Key Properties

- Recognition can occur with or without detailed recollection.
- Familiarity and recollection make different contributions.
- Conscious report may reflect the output of underlying memory processes.

## Construction Or Recognition

Recognize this concept when a recognition task or explanation separates "I know
this" from "I remember where, when or how this occurred."

## Context And Application

The source discusses recognition after hippocampal mechanisms to broaden the
chapter beyond episodic recall alone.

## Examples

Seeing a face and knowing it is familiar without remembering where you met the
person illustrates familiarity without full recollection.

## Common Errors And Common Confusions

- Treating recognition as just partial recall.
- Treating familiarity as the same as recollection.
- Treating conscious report as a direct window onto mechanism.
