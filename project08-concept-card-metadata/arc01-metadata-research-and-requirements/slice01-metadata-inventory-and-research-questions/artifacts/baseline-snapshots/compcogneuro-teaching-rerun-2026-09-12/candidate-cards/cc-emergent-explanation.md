# Concept Card: Emergent Explanation

## Concept Boundary

This card covers the source's account of emergence as explanation across levels.
It excludes a general metaphysics of emergence and any full defense of
reconstructionism beyond the selected passage.

## Quick Definition And Core Definition

Quick definition: emergent explanation explains cognition by showing how higher
level behavior arises from lower level interacting parts without being obvious
from any part alone.

Core definition: the source contrasts reductive explanation with a constructive
approach: build a working system from simpler components and observe how
organized behavior emerges from their interaction. The explanation is not just
"parts cause wholes"; it is the modeled relation between levels.

## Prerequisites And Key Properties

- Multiple levels of description are relevant.
- Component behavior alone does not transparently explain system behavior.
- A model can reconstruct a phenomenon by showing how interactions produce it.

## Construction Or Recognition

Recognize the concept when an explanation asks how system-level cognitive
behavior arises from interacting mechanisms rather than being assigned to a
single component.

## Context And Application

The source uses emergence to motivate cognitive neuroscience modeling: models
can link neurons, networks and cognition without reducing every explanation to
one level.

## Examples

The source uses a gear analogy to distinguish understanding a mechanism from
merely naming parts. The analogy supports the explanatory stance, not a claim
about neural gears.

## Common Errors And Common Confusions

- Confusing emergence with mystery: in the source, emergence is modeled and
  explained, not left magical.
- Collapsing emergence into reduction: the lower level matters, but the
  organized interaction matters too.
- Treating the analogy as evidence: the gear example illustrates the stance; it
  is not neural evidence.
